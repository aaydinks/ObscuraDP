# Targets, how each is defined and measured (Phase 0)

The website publishes four impact targets and two expected outcomes
(`website/src/sections/Impact.astro:5-14`, `website/src/sections/Impact.astro:47-48`). This file gives each one a definition
precise enough to check, says how it will be measured, and states where it stands today. The proposed
definitions are **decisions for the Phase 0 gate**.

In this file, "budget" always means the total privacy budget $\varepsilon$ for a whole training run, with
$\delta = 10^{-5}$, counted per record, unless stated otherwise.

## Reference table 1: what a correct total budget costs in noise

Today the code solves the noise level for one round and reuses it for 20 rounds (finding G2). The table
shows the noise multiplier $\sigma$ needed when the budget covers the whole run: 20 rounds, 3 local epochs,
batch 32, Poisson sampling, $\delta = 10^{-5}$. Computed with `dp-accounting 0.6.0`. Each
cell is "PLD accountant / RDP accountant"; PLD is the tighter of the two. Since S7 the library reproduces
every PLD cell (`tests/test_accounting.py`); one cell moved by 0.02 when the library's own search replaced
the Phase 0 script (stroke, 393 rows, $\varepsilon = 0.5$: 15.67 became 15.65, still within budget).

| Participant (real rows) | Sampling rate $q$ | Steps $T$ | $\varepsilon = 0.5$ | $\varepsilon = 1$ | $\varepsilon = 2$ | $\varepsilon = 4$ | $\varepsilon = 8$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stroke, 220 rows | 0.1455 | 420 | 21.05 / 22.98 | 11.22 / 12.17 | 6.05 / 6.53 | 3.36 / 3.59 | 1.96 / 2.08 |
| Stroke, 393 rows | 0.0814 | 740 | 15.65 / 17.07 | 8.35 / 9.06 | 4.53 / 4.88 | 2.54 / 2.72 | 1.53 / 1.62 |
| Stroke, 2,482 rows | 0.0129 | 4,660 | 6.26 / 6.82 | 3.38 / 3.66 | 1.91 / 2.04 | 1.18 / 1.25 | 0.83 / 0.87 |
| Loan, 683 rows | 0.0469 | 1,300 | 11.94 / 13.04 | 6.39 / 6.93 | 3.49 / 3.75 | 1.99 / 2.12 | 1.24 / 1.31 |

For comparison, the committed stroke runs labelled "ε = 9" used noise multipliers between about 0.50 and
0.78 (see `docs/ROADMAP_STATUS.md`). A correct total budget of 1 needs 4 to 20 times more noise than that.

What this means in practice:
- Expect accuracy at small budgets to drop sharply once accounting is corrected (S11), most of all for small
  participants.
- The PLD accountant saves about 7 to 9% of noise against RDP, for free.
- At a fixed budget, changing the batch size or the number of local steps moves the total injected noise
  very little. The levers that do help are smaller models, pooling noise across participants (the distributed
  mode, S12) and starting from a publicly trained model.
- Row counts are the real ones, not the resampled ones. The sizes are examples from the seed-42 stroke split.

## Reference table 2: bits on the wire for a masked integer sum

Each value is rounded to $b$ bits. To add $N$ of them without overflow the wire width is
$k = b + \lceil \log_2 N \rceil$ bits. Plain federated learning sends 32 bits per value.

| Participants $N$ | $b = 6$ | $b = 8$ | $b = 12$ | $b = 16$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 9 bits, 0.28 | 11 bits, 0.34 | 15 bits, 0.47 | 19 bits, 0.59 |
| 10 | 10 bits, 0.31 | 12 bits, 0.38 | 16 bits, 0.50 | 20 bits, 0.63 |
| 256 | 14 bits, 0.44 | 16 bits, 0.50 | 20 bits, 0.63 | 24 bits, 0.75 |

Each cell is "wire bits per value, ratio to 32-bit". A ratio below 0.5 meets the target before message
framing is counted, so the working rule is $k \le 14$, that is $b \le 14 - \lceil \log_2 N \rceil$.

What this means:
- Pairwise masking with 8-bit to 12-bit values meets the bytes target at 5 sites. Masking itself adds no
  bytes per round; key setup is a one-time cost of a few dozen bytes per peer.
- 16-bit values, which the original API sketch used, do not meet it.
- The distributed integer-noise path needs room for the noise, so it probably lands between 14 and 20 bits
  and is borderline or over. To be measured in S14.
- CKKS cannot meet it: measured 14.4 times larger. Exact BFV is expected to be a few times larger, to be
  measured.
- Whether rounding to 8 bits costs accuracy is an empirical question for S14. Rounding after the noise is
  added does not weaken the privacy guarantee in the local mode, where every message carries all of its
  noise. In the shared-noise mode (S12) a message carries only a slice, values must never be cut off, and
  the rounding step is tied to the size of a slice; see `docs/SHARED_NOISE.md`.
- Measured in S12 for the shared-noise mode with floating-point noise: 12 bits per number for three
  participants and 14 bits for the five stroke-sized participants at a total budget of 1 (ratios 0.38 and
  0.44).
- Measured in S14 over the whole comparison (`docs/PHASE3_RESULTS.md`), per round against 32-bit numbers:
  local noise through the secure sum 0.47 to 0.48 (12-bit values) and 0.34 to 0.36 (8-bit values); shared
  ordinary noise 0.38 to 0.41; shared whole-number noise 0.57 to 0.69; whole-participant mode 0.31 to
  0.32; CKKS 17 times plain for the stroke network and 59 times for the loan network. **Over a whole run
  the shared-noise modes upload more than local noise** (2 to 14 times), because they need 60 to 600
  rounds where local noise needs 20. The target is worded per round; the website must not suggest fewer
  bytes in total for those modes.
- Answer to the open question of S1 (does rounding to 8 bits cost accuracy): in one of eight cells, yes
  (stroke network at a budget of 1: 0.022 AUC, interval 0.003 to 0.041); nowhere with 12 bits.
- Measured in S13 for the shared-noise mode with whole-number noise (five participants, total budget 1,
  20 to 100 full rounds): **18 to 22 bits per number (ratios 0.56 to 0.69)** when the range covers the
  largest sum that is possible at all. With a declared limit of 30 clipping norms per number
  (`sum_bound`), 15 to 19 bits (0.47 to 0.59); only logistic regression gets to 0.5. So the whole-number
  path **does not meet the bytes target** as it stands. The earlier estimate of 14 to 20 bits was about
  right. The reason is the grid: it must be fine enough that rounding a record barely lengthens it, and
  the noise, which is large in full rounds, is then many grid points wide. Whether a coarser grid (more
  noise, fewer bits) is the better trade is part of S14.

## Reference table 3: three numbers for the attacker-advantage target

"Advantage" is the attacker's true-positive rate minus false-positive rate when guessing whether a record
was in the training data. 0 means no better than a coin flip.

1. **General ceiling**, true for any method with this budget:
   $\dfrac{e^{\varepsilon} - 1 + 2\delta}{e^{\varepsilon} + 1}$.
2. **Ceiling for this noise method**: the largest advantage any attacker can have against DP-SGD calibrated
   to exactly this budget. Computed here with the PLD accountant for the loan setting (683 rows, 1,300
   steps); it varies a little with the setting.
3. **Measured**: what a named attack actually achieves. Not available until S15.

| Budget $\varepsilon$ | General ceiling | Ceiling for this noise method | Measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 24.5% | 5.6% | S15 |
| 1 | 46.2% | 10.6% | S15 |
| 2 | 76.2% | 19.5% | S15 |
| 4 | 96.4% | 34.6% | S15 |
| 8 | 99.9% | 56.3% | S15 |

What this means:
- The website sentence "at most 52.5% of the time" at a budget of 1 is not a guarantee
  (`website/src/sections/Impact.astro:10`). The general limit on attacker accuracy is 73.1%.
- A guaranteed 5% advantage under the general ceiling needs a budget of about 0.10.
- With this noise method the ceiling at a budget of 1 is about 10.6%, and it reaches 5% at a budget a
  little below 0.5. So "at most 5% at a budget of 1" can only ever be a **measured** result against a specific attack,
  and a strong attack that sees every training step may legitimately measure more than 5%.
- Proposed wording for the target: "In our published attack tests, measured attacker advantage at a total
  budget of 1 is at most 5%, shown next to the proven worst case." The gate decides whether to keep the
  budget at 1 or move the target to a budget where 5% is provable.

## Engineering measures to track from Phase 1 on

- CI green on `main`.
- Coverage of core modules at least 85% at release (80% at the end of Phase 1).
- Zero unsafe flags in the default plan.
- Accountant cross-check inside its band on every run.
- Comparison tests against the old code unchanged unless a pull request explains why.
- Documentation snippets executed in CI.
- Timed clean install and quickstart.
- No network access during tests (backs the no-telemetry promise).
